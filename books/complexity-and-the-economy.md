# Complexity and the Economy

W. Brian Arthur — 2014

## Three key theories

1. **Increasing returns and lock-in** — Where returns rise with adoption, small historical events tip markets toward one technology. Outcomes are path-dependent, potentially inefficient and unpredictable in advance. Multiple equilibria replace a single optimum.
2. **Inductive reasoning** — Agents in ill-defined situations cannot deduce; they form, test and drop hypotheses. The El Farol bar problem and the Santa Fe artificial stock market show such agents producing persistent non-equilibrium dynamics, bubbles and crashes.
3. **Economy out of equilibrium** — Complexity economics studies how agents react to the outcome patterns they jointly create. The economy is always forming, not at rest. Technology evolves by combining existing elements, and the economy restructures around it.
