# At Home in the Universe

Stuart Kauffman — 1995

## Three key theories

1. **Order for free** — Self-organization, not only natural selection, creates biological order. Random Boolean networks of genes settle into a small number of stable attractors, which Kauffman links to cell types. Selection works on order that already exists.
2. **Autocatalytic sets and the origin of life** — In a diverse enough soup of molecules, sets that catalyze each other's formation arise almost inevitably. Life then emerges as a phase transition, not a lucky accident. Kauffman argues we are "at home in the universe," expected rather than improbable.
3. **Fitness landscapes and the edge of chaos** — The NK model tunes how rugged a fitness landscape is through the interactions between parts. Too few interactions give frozen order; too many give chaos. Evolving and coevolving systems perform best at the edge of chaos, a pattern Kauffman extends to technology and organizations.
