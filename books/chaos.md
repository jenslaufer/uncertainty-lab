# Chaos

James Gleick — 1987

## Three key theories

1. **Sensitive dependence on initial conditions** — Edward Lorenz found that tiny rounding differences in a simple weather model produced wholly different forecasts — the butterfly effect. Deterministic systems can be unpredictable in practice. Long-range forecasting has hard limits.
2. **Universality** — Mitchell Feigenbaum found that many different systems approach chaos through period doubling at the same ratio. The route to chaos does not depend on the details of the system. Simple models can capture real behavior.
3. **Fractals and strange attractors** — Benoit Mandelbrot showed that irregular shapes, from coastlines to cotton prices, repeat their structure across scales. Chaotic systems trace strange attractors with this fractal geometry. Disorder carries hidden order.
