# The Evolution of Cooperation

Robert Axelrod — 1984

## Three key theories

1. **TIT FOR TAT wins the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma** — In Axelrod's computer tournaments, the simplest entry won: cooperate first, then copy the opponent's last move. Successful strategies were nice, retaliatory, forgiving, and clear. Cooperation pays without trust or central authority.
2. **The shadow of the future** — Cooperation is stable only when future interactions weigh enough against the present. A high enough chance of meeting again makes defection unprofitable. Enlarge the shadow of the future and you promote cooperation.
3. **Cooperation invades through clusters** — A world of unconditional defectors cannot be invaded by single cooperators, but a small cluster of reciprocators that interact mostly with each other can. Once established, reciprocity resists invasion. Cooperation can emerge and ratchet up, as in the trench-war "live and let live" system.
