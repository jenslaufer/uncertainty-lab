# Introduction to Probability

Joseph Blitzstein & Jessica Hwang — 2nd ed., 2019

## Three key theories

1. **Conditioning** — The authors call conditioning the soul of statistics. Bayes' rule, the law of total probability and first-step analysis turn hard problems into chains of conditional ones. Conditioning is how you update beliefs on evidence.
2. **Stories over formulas** — Each distribution comes with a story of how it arises: Binomial as counted successes, Poisson as rare events, Exponential as memoryless waiting. Story proofs replace algebra with counting arguments. Stories reveal the connections between distributions.
3. **Limit theorems and long-run behavior** — The law of large numbers and the central limit theorem explain why averages stabilize and look Normal. Markov chains add long-run behavior for dependent sequences through stationary distributions. Together they tie single random events to predictable aggregates.
