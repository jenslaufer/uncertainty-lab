# Gödel, Escher, Bach

Douglas Hofstadter — 1979

## Three key theories

1. **Strange loops and tangled hierarchies** — A strange loop moves up or down a hierarchy of levels and lands where it started. Gödel's self-referential sentence, Escher's drawings and Bach's endlessly rising canon share this structure. Hofstadter argues the self is such a loop.
2. **Formal systems, isomorphism and incompleteness** — Meaningless symbols gain meaning through isomorphism with something outside them. Gödel showed that any sufficiently strong formal system can encode statements about itself, so it contains true statements it cannot prove. Rules alone never capture all truth.
3. **Mind emerges across levels** — Thought arises from low-level activity that does not think, as the "Aunt Hillary" ant colony shows. Meaning lives at the high level of active symbols, not in neurons. Hence the case that a mind could, in principle, run on a different substrate.
