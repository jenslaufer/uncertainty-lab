# Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms

David MacKay — 2003

## Three key theories

1. **Information theory and inference are one subject** — MacKay treats compression, communication and learning as the same problem: modeling probability distributions. Better models give better compression and better decoding. The book teaches both sides together.
2. **Bayesian inference and Occam's razor** — Probability is the calculus of reasoning under uncertainty. Bayesian model comparison through the evidence penalizes overly complex models on its own, an automatic Occam's razor. Monte Carlo and variational methods make this inference tractable.
3. **Noisy-channel coding with sparse-graph codes** — Shannon's theorem says reliable communication up to capacity is possible. MacKay shows how modern codes, such as low-density parity-check and turbo codes, approach capacity with message-passing decoders. Neural networks appear in the same frame, as learning algorithms that perform inference.
