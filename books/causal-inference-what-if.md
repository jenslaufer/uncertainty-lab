# Causal Inference: What If

Miguel Hernán & James Robins — 2020

## Three key theories

1. **Counterfactual outcomes and the target trial** — A causal effect compares potential outcomes under different treatments. Every observational analysis should emulate a hypothetical randomized trial, the target trial, with explicit eligibility, interventions and time zero.
2. **Identifiability conditions** — Causal effects can be estimated from observational data only under exchangeability, positivity and consistency. Causal diagrams make these assumptions explicit and reveal confounding and selection bias.
3. **G-methods for time-varying treatments** — When treatment and confounders change over time, standard regression fails. Inverse probability weighting, the g-formula (standardization) and g-estimation of structural nested models give valid estimates.
