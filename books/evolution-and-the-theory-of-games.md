# Evolution and the Theory of Games

John Maynard Smith — 1982

## Three key theories

1. **Evolutionarily stable strategy (ESS)** — A strategy is an ESS if, once common, no rare mutant strategy can invade it. Natural selection replaces rational choice; payoffs are fitness. The concept predicts stable behavior in populations without assuming foresight.
2. **The Hawk–Dove game** — Escalating fighters (Hawks) and displaying retreaters (Doves) compete for a resource. When injury costs exceed the resource's value, the stable state is a mixture of both. It explains why animal contests are often ritualized rather than lethal.
3. **Asymmetric contests and the Bourgeois strategy** — An arbitrary asymmetry, such as who owns a territory, can settle conflicts without fighting. "Fight if owner, retreat if intruder" is an ESS. Conventions emerge from uncorrelated asymmetries, not from differences in strength.
