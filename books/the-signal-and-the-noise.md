# The Signal and the Noise

Nate Silver — 2012

## Three key theories

1. **Mistaking noise for signal** — More data brings more noise, and forecasters overfit it, finding patterns that are not there. Failures in economics, earthquakes, and the 2008 crisis show overconfidence in weak signals. Good forecasters respect what cannot be predicted.
2. **Think like a Bayesian** — State a prior, then update it in proportion to the evidence. Silver presents Bayes' theorem as the method for approaching truth step by step. Forecasts should be probabilistic and calibrated, not point predictions.
3. **Foxes vs. hedgehogs** — Borrowing Tetlock's distinction, Silver argues that foxes, who weigh many approaches and revise readily, forecast better than hedgehogs with one big idea. Weather forecasting improved by combining models with human judgment. Humility and iteration beat ideology.
