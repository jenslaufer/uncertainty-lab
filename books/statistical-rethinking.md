# Statistical Rethinking

Richard McElreath — 2nd ed., 2020

## Three key theories

1. **Bayesian inference as counting (small world vs. large world)** — The posterior counts the ways each conjecture could produce the data, weighted by prior plausibility. Models are "golems": powerful but mindless, valid only inside their small world. The large world demands judgment the golem cannot supply.
2. **Causal inference with DAGs** — Regression alone does not reveal causes; adding variables can create bias as easily as remove it. Directed acyclic graphs expose forks, pipes, and colliders, and the backdoor criterion tells you what to condition on. The causal model must come before the statistical one.
3. **Multilevel models with partial pooling** — Varying-effects models remember what they learn about each cluster and share it across clusters. Partial pooling shrinks noisy estimates toward the population and beats both complete and no pooling. McElreath argues multilevel regression deserves to be the default.
