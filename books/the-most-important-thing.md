# The Most Important Thing

Howard Marks — 2011

## Three key theories

1. **Second-level thinking** — First-level thinking says "good company, buy." Second-level thinking asks what the consensus expects, how the price reflects it and why the consensus might be wrong. Superior returns require different and better views than the crowd.
2. **Risk is the chance of permanent loss** — Risk is not volatility; it is the probability of losing money, and it cannot be measured in advance. Risk is highest when people believe it is low and prices rise. The future is a range of possible outcomes; only one occurs.
3. **Cycles and the pendulum** — Markets and investor psychology swing between greed and fear, optimism and pessimism. Few things move in straight lines, and excess in one direction breeds the reverse. You cannot predict, but you can prepare: know where you stand in the cycle and act against the pendulum.
