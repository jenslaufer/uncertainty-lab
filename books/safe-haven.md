# Safe Haven

Mark Spitznagel — 2021

## Three key theories

1. **The volatility tax** — Investors compound; they earn the geometric average, not the arithmetic one. Large losses drag the geometric average far below the arithmetic. The book reframes risk mitigation as the job of raising compound growth, not of lowering volatility for its own sake.
2. **Cost-effective risk mitigation** — Spitznagel sorts safe havens into store of value, alpha and insurance. Most "safe" assets cost more in lost returns than they save in crashes. A small, highly convex insurance payoff in a crash can raise a portfolio's long-run compound return.
3. **Bernoulli and the geometric average** — Spitznagel devotes a chapter to Daniel Bernoulli's 1738 paper. He reads it as a case for judging risky bets by their geometric average, not their arithmetic expected value. An investor with one unrepeatable sequence of returns is a punter with a sample size of one, not the house that repeats a bet many times.
