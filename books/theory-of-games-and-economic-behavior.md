# Theory of Games and Economic Behavior

John von Neumann & Oskar Morgenstern — 1944

## Three key theories

1. **Expected utility** — Preferences that satisfy a set of axioms can be represented by a numerical utility, and rational choice under risk maximizes its expected value. This made utility measurable and founded decision theory under uncertainty.
2. **Two-person zero-sum games and the minimax theorem** — Every finite two-person zero-sum game has a value when players may use mixed strategies. Each player can guarantee it by minimizing the maximum loss. Randomization becomes a rational strategy.
3. **Cooperative n-person games** — With more players, coalitions decide outcomes. The characteristic function gives what each coalition can secure; stable sets ("solutions") describe imputations that no coalition can profitably block. Economics becomes a study of strategic interaction, not isolated optimization.
