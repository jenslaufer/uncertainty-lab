# Elements of Information Theory

Thomas Cover & Joy Thomas — 2nd ed., 2006

## Three key theories

1. **Entropy, relative entropy and mutual information** — Entropy measures the uncertainty of a random variable; relative entropy (KL divergence) measures the distance between distributions; mutual information measures what one variable reveals about another. These quantities obey chain rules and inequalities that structure the whole field. The asymptotic equipartition property turns them into counts of typical sequences.
2. **Source coding** — Entropy is the limit of lossless compression: no code can beat it on average, and Huffman and arithmetic codes approach it. Rate–distortion theory extends this to lossy compression. Data compression and probability estimation turn out to be the same problem.
3. **Channel capacity** — Every noisy channel has a capacity, the maximum mutual information between input and output. Below capacity, error-free communication is possible; above it, impossible. The book applies the same tools to gambling and portfolio theory, where the growth rate of wealth equals mutual information (the Kelly link).
