# Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos

Steven Strogatz — 2nd ed., 2015

## Three key theories

1. **The geometric view of dynamics** — Instead of solving equations, draw the flow: fixed points, their stability, trajectories in the phase plane, limit cycles. Qualitative analysis reveals long-run behavior that formulas hide. Linearization near fixed points classifies their local behavior.
2. **Bifurcations** — As a parameter changes, fixed points appear, collide and change stability: saddle-node, transcritical, pitchfork and Hopf bifurcations. Small parameter shifts can switch a system's behavior abruptly, with hysteresis. Applications range from lasers to insect outbreaks.
3. **Chaos and strange attractors** — Deterministic systems with three or more dimensions, like the Lorenz equations, can show aperiodic behavior with sensitive dependence on initial conditions. One-dimensional maps reach chaos through period doubling, with Feigenbaum's universal constants. Strange attractors have fractal structure, measured by fractal dimension and Lyapunov exponents.
