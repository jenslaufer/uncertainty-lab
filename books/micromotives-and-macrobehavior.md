# Micromotives and Macrobehavior

Thomas Schelling — 1978

## Three key theories

1. **Micromotives do not predict macrobehavior** — Aggregate outcomes arise from individuals responding to each other and to an environment others shape. The collective result can match no one's intention. You cannot read individual preferences off the pattern, nor the pattern off the preferences.
2. **Sorting and mixing (the segregation model)** — Mild preferences for not being in a local minority, applied move by move on a checkerboard, produce near-total segregation. Each relocation changes the neighborhood for others and triggers further moves. Small biases compound into stark sorting.
3. **Critical mass and tipping** — Many behaviors depend on how many others already do them. Once participation crosses a threshold, it becomes self-sustaining; below it, it collapses. Neighborhoods tip, seminars die, and conventions take hold through this feedback.
