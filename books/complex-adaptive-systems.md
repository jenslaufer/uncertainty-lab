# Complex Adaptive Systems

John Miller & Scott Page — 2007

## Three key theories

1. **Computation as theory** — Agent-based computational models are a legitimate form of theory, between verbal argument and closed-form mathematics. They force precise assumptions yet let you study heterogeneous, adaptive agents that equations cannot handle. The model becomes an artificial world you can rerun and vary.
2. **Emergence from local interaction** — Adaptive agents following simple local rules produce aggregate patterns no agent intends or controls. The whole cannot be read off the parts. Schelling's segregation model is a classic case.
3. **Social science "in between"** — The interesting cases lie between the extremes standard models handle well: between simple and strategic behavior, between pairs and infinities of agents, between equilibrium and chaos. Computational models also sit between richness and rigor. The authors argue social science should work in this middle ground.
