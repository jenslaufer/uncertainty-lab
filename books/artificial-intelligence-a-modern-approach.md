# Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach

Stuart Russell & Peter Norvig — 4th ed., 2020

## Three key theories

1. **The rational agent** — AI is the study of agents that perceive their environment and act to maximize expected performance. Environments differ in observability, determinism, and the presence of other agents, and agent designs follow from these properties. The framing unifies search, logic, planning, and learning.
2. **Reasoning and deciding under uncertainty** — Agents represent uncertainty with probability theory, compactly via Bayesian networks. Decision theory combines beliefs with utilities; Markov decision processes extend this to sequential choice. Rational action means maximizing expected utility given beliefs.
3. **Beyond the standard model: uncertain objectives** — The 4th edition argues the standard model, optimizing a fixed objective, fails when the objective is misspecified. Machines should instead be uncertain about human preferences and learn them from behavior. This reframes AI as assistance games with humans.
