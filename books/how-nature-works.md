# How Nature Works

Per Bak — 1996

## Three key theories

1. **Self-organized criticality** — Large driven systems evolve on their own to a critical state, with no fine-tuning. At that state, events of all sizes occur. Bak proposes this as a general mechanism behind complexity in nature.
2. **The sandpile model** — Add grains one at a time and the pile reaches a critical slope. Avalanches then follow a power-law size distribution: the same grain can trigger a slide of one grain or of the whole pile. Catastrophes need no special cause.
3. **Punctuated equilibrium in evolution** — In the Bak–Sneppen model, the least fit species mutates and disturbs its neighbours. The ecosystem self-organizes into long calm periods broken by extinction avalanches of all sizes. The same logic explains earthquakes (Gutenberg–Richter law), 1/f noise and market fluctuations.
