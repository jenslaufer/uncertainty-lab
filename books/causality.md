# Causality

Judea Pearl — 2nd ed., 2009

## Three key theories

1. **Structural causal models** — Causal knowledge lives in structural equations and their graph, not in joint distributions. Bayesian networks gain causal meaning when arrows denote mechanisms that interventions can replace. One model answers associational, interventional and counterfactual queries.
2. **Interventions and identification** — The do-operator formalizes intervention as surgery on the graph. The do-calculus, with back-door and front-door criteria, decides when a causal effect is identifiable from observational data. d-separation links graph structure to conditional independence.
3. **Counterfactuals** — Structural models define counterfactuals at the unit level. This yields probabilities of causation (necessity, sufficiency), direct and indirect effects, and a formal account of actual cause.
