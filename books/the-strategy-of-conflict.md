# The Strategy of Conflict

Thomas Schelling — 1960

## Three key theories

1. **Focal points and tacit coordination** — Players who cannot communicate still coordinate by converging on a salient solution both expect the other to expect. Salience comes from precedent, symmetry, or prominence, not from the payoffs alone. Mixed-motive bargaining often settles on such focal points.
2. **Commitment and strategic moves** — A player gains power by visibly limiting his own options, so the opponent must adjust. Threats and promises work only when made credible, often by burning bridges or delegating. Weakness and irreversibility become sources of bargaining strength.
3. **The threat that leaves something to chance** — A threat too costly to carry out deliberately becomes credible when it creates a risk of mutual disaster that no one fully controls. Brinkmanship is the deliberate manipulation of shared risk. This explains deterrence and limited war under nuclear weapons.
