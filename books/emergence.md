# Emergence

John Holland — 1998

## Three key theories

1. **Much coming from little** — A few simple rules can generate vast complexity. Board games such as checkers and Go show this: a handful of rules yields more positions than can ever be explored. Emergence happens in rule-governed systems, and the whole exceeds the sum of its parts.
2. **Constrained generating procedures** — Holland proposes a formal framework for emergence: mechanisms that interact under constraints, linked into networks. Cellular automata, neural networks and Samuel's self-improving checkers program are examples. The framework aims to make emergence precise enough for scientific study.
3. **Models, building blocks and levels** — Holland studies emergence through models that keep a few building blocks and the rules that combine them. Persistent emergent patterns, such as gliders in the Game of Life, obey rules of their own and define a new level. A model at that level treats them as building blocks in turn.
