# Why Stock Markets Crash

Didier Sornette — 2003

## Three key theories

1. **Crashes as critical points** — A crash is not a random shock but the climax of a bubble that matures endogenously. The market approaches a critical point, like a physical system near a phase transition. Small news then triggers a large collapse, because the system is primed for it.
2. **Imitation and herding** — Traders copy their neighbours. Local imitation builds long-range correlations that drive prices up faster than exponentially. Positive feedback, not fundamentals, inflates the bubble.
3. **Log-periodic power laws** — Near the critical point, prices follow a power law decorated with accelerating log-periodic oscillations, a signature of discrete scale invariance. Sornette fits this pattern to past bubbles and uses it to estimate the most probable crash time, framed as a probability, not a certainty.
