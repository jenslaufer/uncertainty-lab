# Fooled by Randomness

Nassim Taleb — 2001

## Three key theories

1. **Alternative histories** — Judge a decision by the outcomes it could have produced, not the one that occurred. A Russian-roulette winner is rich but not skilled. Monte Carlo thinking exposes luck that results hide.
2. **Survivorship bias** — We see the winners and miss the graveyard of those who took the same bets and failed. Successful traders and managers look skilled because the failures dropped out. Track records mislead when the losers vanish from the sample.
3. **Skewness: frequency vs. magnitude** — Being right often matters less than how much you win or lose when you are right or wrong. A strategy can win most of the time and still blow up on one rare event. Expectation, not probability of success, should drive the bet.
