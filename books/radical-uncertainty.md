# Radical Uncertainty

John Kay & Mervyn King — 2020

## Three key theories

1. **Resolvable vs. radical uncertainty** — Resolvable uncertainty can be removed by looking something up or assigned a probability. Radical uncertainty cannot: we do not know the full set of outcomes. Puzzles have solutions; mysteries do not.
2. **"What is going on here?"** — Under radical uncertainty people reason through narratives, not probabilities. The key question is "What is going on here?". Good decisions start from a credible account of the situation.
3. **Small worlds vs. large worlds** — Optimizing models work in Savage's small worlds, where all states are known. Applying them to large worlds produces false precision. Kay and King favor reference narratives and strategies that are robust and resilient.
