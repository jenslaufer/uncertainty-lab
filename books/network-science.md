# Network Science

Albert-László Barabási — 2016

## Three key theories

1. **Random networks as baseline** — The Erdős–Rényi model predicts a Poisson degree distribution, a giant component above a critical average degree, and short paths. It explains small worlds but fails to produce the hubs seen in real networks. It serves as the null model.
2. **Scale-free property and the Barabási–Albert model** — Real networks show power-law degree distributions. Growth plus preferential attachment produces them; the Bianconi–Barabási fitness model extends this. Degree exponents determine whether moments diverge and how the network behaves.
3. **Robustness and spreading on heterogeneous networks** — Hubs make scale-free networks resilient to random failure and vulnerable to attack. In epidemic models the hubs drive the epidemic threshold toward zero, so even weak contagions can spread. Structure, not just transmission rate, sets outcomes.
