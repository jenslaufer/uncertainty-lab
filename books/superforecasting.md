# Superforecasting

Philip Tetlock & Dan Gardner — 2015

## Three key theories

1. **Forecasting is a measurable, learnable skill** — In the Good Judgment Project, a small group of "superforecasters" beat other teams, including intelligence analysts, year after year. Brier scores measured accuracy and calibration. Skill, not luck, explains their edge, and training improves it.
2. **Outside view first, then inside view, then update** — Superforecasters break questions into parts ("Fermi-izing"), anchor on base rates, then adjust for specifics. They update often and in small increments. They treat beliefs as hypotheses to test, a mindset Tetlock calls "perpetual beta".
3. **Foxes beat hedgehogs; teams and aggregation beat individuals** — Open-minded, multi-perspective thinkers forecast better than big-idea experts. Teams that challenge each other outperform individuals. Aggregating many forecasts, and extremizing the average, sharpens accuracy further.
