# The Information

James Gleick — 2011

## Three key theories

1. **Information separated from meaning** — Claude Shannon defined information as a measurable quantity — the resolution of uncertainty, counted in bits — independent of what a message means. This abstraction made modern communication and computing possible. Gleick traces the path from African talking drums and the telegraph to Shannon.
2. **Information is physical** — Maxwell's demon, Szilard and Landauer link information to thermodynamic entropy: erasing a bit costs energy. Genes turn out to be codes, and physicists such as John Wheeler argue "it from bit": information as the basis of physics. Information becomes a fundamental quantity, not a metaphor.
3. **The flood** — As information becomes cheap, abundance replaces scarcity. Meaning, filtering and search become the bottleneck. Gleick uses Borges' Library of Babel and Wikipedia to show that more information does not mean more knowledge.
