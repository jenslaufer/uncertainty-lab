# An Introduction to Probability Theory and Its Applications

William Feller — Vol. 1, 3rd ed., 1968

## Three key theories

1. **Fluctuations in coin tossing** — Coin-tossing and random walks defy intuition. The arc sine law shows that in a long game one player is likely to lead almost the whole time, and returns to a tie are far rarer than expected. The fraction of time one side leads does not settle near one half.
2. **Recurrent events and renewal theory** — Feller treats patterns that repeat, such as returns to the origin, as recurrent events. Generating functions turn their waiting-time problems into algebra. A general limit theorem then gives long-run renewal rates.
3. **Random walks, ruin and Markov chains** — The gambler's ruin problem yields the probability of ruin and the expected duration of a game. Markov chains generalize the random walk. Feller classifies states as persistent or transient and derives long-run frequencies through an ergodic theorem.
