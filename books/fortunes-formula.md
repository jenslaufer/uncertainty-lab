# Fortune's Formula

William Poundstone — 2005

## Three key theories

1. **The Kelly criterion** — John Kelly showed in 1956 that a gambler with an edge maximizes long-run wealth growth by betting a fixed fraction of capital: edge divided by odds. The growth rate equals the information rate of the gambler's private wire. Information theory becomes a theory of betting.
2. **Kelly versus Samuelson** — Paul Samuelson and other economists attacked Kelly: maximizing log growth is just one utility function, not a rule for rational investors. Thorp and Shannon defended it as the strategy that, over time, almost surely beats any essentially different strategy. The book frames this feud as the core dispute between information theorists and academic finance.
3. **Overbetting leads to ruin** — Betting more than the Kelly fraction raises risk and lowers growth; past twice Kelly, growth turns negative. Poundstone uses Long-Term Capital Management as the case of leverage that ignored this limit. Fractional Kelly trades some growth for a much smoother path.
