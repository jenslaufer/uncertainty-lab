# Six Degrees

Duncan Watts — 2003

## Three key theories

1. **Small-world networks** — A few random long-range shortcuts in a highly clustered network collapse path lengths while local clustering stays high. This Watts–Strogatz model explains how a world of tight groups is still only a few steps across.
2. **Searchability** — Short paths exist, but people must also find them without a global map. Milgram's senders succeeded because social identity is organized along several dimensions (location, occupation), letting each step move closer to the target.
3. **Cascades and thresholds** — When individuals adopt a behavior once enough neighbors do, systems sit quiet for long stretches and then tip into global cascades. Outcome depends on network structure more than on the initial trigger. Systems are robust yet fragile, and the size of a cascade is hard to predict.
